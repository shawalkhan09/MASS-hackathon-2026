### Step 4: Applicable Frameworks

#### 1. Root Cause Analysis (5 Whys)
*   **Definition:** A simple, iterative questioning technique that traces a problem back through its chain of causes by repeatedly asking “why” — typically five times — until an underlying, fixable root cause is reached, rather than stopping at the first visible symptom. It originated at Toyota and is widely used in Lean and Six Sigma.
*   **When to Use It:** Best suited to a single, moderately complex problem with a fairly linear cause-and-effect chain. It works well as a quick, low-cost first pass before deploying heavier tools.
*   **Justification:** This framework is essential for peeling back the layers from the "proximate cause" (faulty sensor data) to the "systemic root causes" (cost-driven organizational decisions and weak oversight) that Boeing experienced.

#### 2. Fishbone (Ishikawa) Diagram
*   **Definition:** A visualization tool used to identify and organize the many potential causes of a problem, categorized by source (e.g., People, Process, Equipment, Materials, Environment, Measurement). It helps teams move beyond superficial explanations and see how different factors contribute to an outcome.
*   **When to Use It:** Use when a problem likely has multiple, interacting causes across different areas rather than one obvious linear chain. It is often paired with 5 Whys to drill deeper into each branch once causes are mapped.
*   **Justification:** The 737 MAX crisis involved complex, interacting failures across engineering (equipment), corporate culture (people/process), and the FAA relationship (regulatory/environment). A Fishbone diagram is the best tool to map these distinct categories into a holistic view of the failure.

#### 3. Pareto Analysis (80/20 Rule)
*   **Definition:** A quantitative decision-making technique based on the principle that 80% of effects often result from 20% of causes. The process involves identifying and ranking problems by their contribution (e.g., dollars lost) to focus corrective action on the "vital few" issues.
*   **When to Use It:** When you have multiple issues contributing to a large financial or operational impact and need to prioritize where to allocate remediation efforts or capital.
*   **Justification:** With estimated costs reaching $20 billion, Boeing had to prioritize which failures (e.g., software design, training protocols, fleet inspection) were the most critical to resolve to secure regulatory return-to-service, making Pareto analysis highly relevant for understanding where the "vital few" fixes were required.
