To analyze Peloton’s inventory and demand collapse, the following diagnostic frameworks provide the necessary structure to untangle the operational failures and financial consequences:

### 1. Root Cause Analysis (5 Whys)
*   **Definition:** A simple, iterative questioning technique used to explore the cause-and-effect relationships underlying a particular problem. The goal is to strip away the symptoms to reveal the "root" cause by asking "Why?" at least five times.
*   **When to Use It:** Best suited to a single, moderately complex problem with a fairly linear cause-and-effect chain—such as a specific operational failure—to ensure the analysis reaches the systemic or policy-level cause rather than stopping at the trigger.
*   **Justification:** This helps distinguish the proximate cause (the inventory backlog) from the root cause (flawed demand forecasting methodology and rigid capacity investment decisions).

### 2. Fishbone (Ishikawa) Diagram
*   **Definition:** A visual brainstorming tool that maps every plausible cause of a problem into major categories (e.g., Manpower, Methods, Machines, Materials, Measurements, Environment) branching off a central spine.
*   **When to Use It:** When a problem is multifaceted, involving interacting causes across different areas of the business, or when you need to ensure you haven't overlooked non-obvious contributors.
*   **Justification:** This allows you to categorize the contributing factors—such as supply chain inflexibility (Method/Machine), forecasting bias (Measurement), and the Tread+ recall (Environment)—to visualize how multiple streams of failure converged on the same result.

### 3. Break-even Point (CVP Analysis)
*   **Definition:** A calculation to determine the volume of sales at which total revenues equal total costs (fixed plus variable), resulting in zero profit.
*   **When to Use It:** When assessing the financial risk of high fixed-cost structures, particularly when a business is contemplating or has already committed to heavy investments in capital assets (like factories or acquisitions).
*   **Justification:** Peloton’s shift to a high fixed-cost structure (the $800M+ investment in the Ohio factory and Precor) significantly raised its break-even point, leaving the company structurally unable to sustain itself when sales volume dropped, making this an essential tool for evaluating their strategic error.

### 4. Profitability Framework (Profit Tree)
*   **Definition:** A systematic way to break down a company’s financial performance into its primary drivers: Profit = (Revenue – Costs), where Revenue = (Price × Quantity) and Costs = (Fixed Costs + Variable Costs).
*   **When to Use It:** When you need to quantify the financial impact of a strategic reversal and identify which levers of the business (e.g., volume contraction, expedited freight costs, inventory write-downs) had the largest negative impact on the bottom line.
*   **Justification:** This provides a structured way to quantify how the inventory buildup—and the resulting price-cuts/write-downs—eroded Peloton’s margin, and how the move from owned manufacturing to third-party contract manufacturing intended to re-adjust the Fixed vs. Variable cost ratio.
